Exercise 1
Explain in your own words why the Waterfall method often goes wrong when building software. Give two reasons.   

Reason 1: The customer sees the product only at the very end. If they change their mind, it is too late and very expensive to fix.
Reason 2: Testing happens at the last step. Teams find big bugs too late, so all risks are saved for the end of the project.

Exercise 4
Your team works in two-week sprints. In the middle of a sprint, the CEO comes in and wants a new feature right away. 
What should happen, according to Scrum?   
The team does not change the current Sprint Goal or add new work to the running sprint. 
The Product Owner should add this new idea to the Product Backlog, order it, and plan it for a future sprint. 
BUT in my own practice, things were different: 
we changed the Sprint Goal and the sprint content, moved our current tasks back to the backlog, 
and implemented the new urgent request from the Product Owner.


Exercise 5
What is the difference between the Sprint Review and the Sprint Retrospective? Who takes part in each?   
Sprint Review: Focuses on the product. The team shows the working result to stakeholders and customers to get feedback.   
Who takes part: The Scrum Team (PO, Scrum Master, Developers) and stakeholders/customers.   
Sprint Retrospective: Focuses on the process and teamwork. The team discusses how they worked together and how to improve in the next sprint.   
Who takes part: Only the Scrum Team

Exercise 9
A team's Daily Scrum takes 45 minutes. Everyone stands facing the manager and reports what they did. 
What is wrong, and what would you suggest?   
Wrong: 
- It takes too long (it must be 15 minutes max).
- It is treated like a status report to a boss, but Daily Scrum is a planning meeting for developers.
Suggestions:
- Keep it strictly under 15 minutes.   
- Developers should talk to each other about the Sprint Goal, not report to a manager.
- Use the three standard questions: What did I do? What will I do? What is blocking me?
- Move any long discussions outside the meeting


Exercise 10
Scrum or Kanban? Choose for each team and explain why.
a) A team building a new mobile app: Scrum. 
Sprints help plan releases, gather fast feedback, and build a new product step by step.
b) An IT support desk: Kanban. 
Work comes in unexpectedly all day, so a continuous flow with WIP limits fits better than fixed sprints.
c) A team fixing bugs in a webshop that is already live: Kanban. 
Bugs must be fixed as soon as they appear, so continuous work without waiting for sprint cycles is ideal.


Exercise 11
In planning poker, four developers show 2, 3, 3 and 13 for the same story. What should the team do next?
The team should ask the person with the lowest estimate (2) and the person with the highest estimate (13) to explain their thoughts. 
Usually the person who voted 13 sees extra complexity or hidden risks that others missed or they misunderstood the task. 
After a short discussion, everyone votes again.
